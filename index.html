If you think you have been scammed

  1. Stop replying and do not send more money.
  2. Contact your bank or payment provider through its official channel if money or banking details are involved.
  3. Save relevant evidence, such as transaction references and screenshots, while protecting sensitive information.

Transparency center

What this prototype can — and cannot — do

What it can do

  • Match visible message phrases against simple warning rules.
  • Inspect basic URL structure and selected suspicious patterns.
  • Explain why a warning was raised.
  • Generate a privacy-conscious text receipt locally.
  • Teach safer choices through the Family Shield and quiz.

What it cannot do

  • Guarantee that any message or URL is safe or fraudulent.
  • Check live domain reputation, databases, or threat intelligence.
  • Confirm a sender's identity or verify bank transactions.
  • Detect deepfakes, inspect media, or run a trained AI model.
  • Replace professional advice or an official cybercrime report.

Risk scores are heuristic estimates created by this prototype. A low score is not a safety certificate. Scammers change tactics, and legitimate messages can contain warning words.
